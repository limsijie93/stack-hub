When the right answer is not obvious, build 2-3 competing prototypes or sketches, compare side by side, and only then commit. 'Design it twice' is the same rule by another name.

The load-bearing qualifier is one line: **a second flavour of the first shape does not count.** Alternatives must be structurally distinct, which is what stops the practice degenerating into producing variants to satisfy a process.

Equally valuable is the explicit *when it doesn't apply* section, which most principles omit: mechanical implementation where the pattern is established, bug fixes or refactors with a clear target state, and changes where constraints dictate a single viable approach. Naming the non-applicable cases is what keeps a principle from becoming ceremony.
